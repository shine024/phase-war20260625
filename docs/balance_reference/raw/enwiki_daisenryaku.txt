_**Daisenryaku**_ (大戦略, _**Great Strategy**_) is a series of war [strategy video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategy_video_game "Strategy video game") by SystemSoft and [SystemSoft Alpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SystemSoft_Alpha "SystemSoft Alpha") in Japan. The series debuted in Japan in 1985 with _Gendai Daisenryaku_ (現代大戦略, Modern Great Strategy) exclusively for the [NEC PC-98](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PC-9800_series "PC-9800 series").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisenryaku#cite_note-ascii_20050127-1)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisenryaku#cite_note-:0-2)

Games in the franchise have been released for many platforms including the [PC-88](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PC-8800_series "PC-8800 series"), [X1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X1_(computer) "X1 (computer)"), [FM-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FM-7 "FM-7"), [MSX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSX "MSX"), [Famicom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famicom "Famicom"), [Mega Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mega_Drive "Mega Drive"), [Turbo CD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbo_CD "Turbo CD"), [Game Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Boy "Game Boy"), [Game Gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Gear "Game Gear"), [Super Famicom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Famicom "Super Famicom"), [Sega Saturn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega_Saturn "Sega Saturn"), [Dreamcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreamcast "Dreamcast"), [Game Boy Advance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Boy_Advance "Game Boy Advance"), [Nintendo DS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_DS "Nintendo DS"), [PlayStation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation "PlayStation"), [PlayStation 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_2 "PlayStation 2"), [PlayStation Portable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Portable "PlayStation Portable"), [PlayStation 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_3 "PlayStation 3"), [PlayStation Vita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Vita "PlayStation Vita"), [PlayStation 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4 "PlayStation 4"), [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows "Microsoft Windows"), [Macintosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macintosh "Macintosh") and other mobile devices.

In April 1993, _[Famicom Tsūshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famicom\_Ts%C5%ABshin "Famicom Tsūshin")_ (_Famitsu_) magazine awarded the _Daisenryaku_ series a world record for being ported to the most [game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_consoles "Game consoles"). It was ported to seven consoles up until then, including the Game Boy and Mega Drive.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisenryaku#cite_note-3)

_**Gendai Daisenryaku**_ (現代大戦略, Modern Great Strategy) is the first title in the series, released in November 1985 exclusively for the [NEC PC-98](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PC-9800_series "PC-9800 series").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisenryaku#cite_note-ascii_20050127-1) It sold 20,000 copies.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisenryaku#cite_note-:1-12)

Its successor, _**Daisenryaku II**_, modified game systems including the order of unit's move, the defence statistic of cities, indirect fire, warships, and increased a variety of weapons.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisenryaku#cite_note-:0-2) It sold 50,000 copies.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisenryaku#cite_note-:1-12)_Daisenryaku II_ was too rich and complex for 8-bit computers, so it was ported to them and gaming consoles as a simplified version titled _**Super Daisenryaku**_.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisenryaku#cite_note-:0-2)

_**Daisenryaku III**_ changed the game system from turn-based strategy to real-time strategy. _Daisenryaku_ titles for the PC-98 had been developed for its built-in BASIC interpreter (N88-BASIC (86), a variant of [Microsoft BASIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_BASIC "Microsoft BASIC")). _Daisenryaku III_ was very slow, so it was superseded by the pre-compiled distribution of _Daisenryaku III '90_.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisenryaku#cite_note-13)

_**Advanced Daisenryaku: Deutsch Dengeki Sakusen**_ is a [Mega Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mega_Drive "Mega Drive")[war game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_simulation "Military simulation"). Multiplayer games are possible, using the modem that was sold for the Sega Mega Drive.

_**Daisenryaku Expert WWII: War in Europe**_ (大戦略エキスパートWWII; "Great Strategy Expert WWII") is a [Super Famicom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Nintendo_Entertainment_System "Super Nintendo Entertainment System") game that takes place during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") at either the [Eastern Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_(World_War_II) "Eastern Front (World War II)") or the [Western Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Front_(World_War_II) "Western Front (World War II)"). There is a [scenario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scenario "Scenario") mode and a [campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_campaign "Military campaign") mode. By default, the player controls the Third Reich but it can be changed prior to starting the game.

An remake of the game was released in 2006 for the PlayStation 2 as a part of the [Sega Ages 2500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega_Ages_2500 "Sega Ages 2500") series.

_**Iron Storm**_ is the 1996 [North American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_America "North America") release of _**World Advanced Daisenryaku: Kōtetsu no Senpū**_ (ワールドアドバンスド大戦略 〜鋼鉄の戦風〜) for the [Sega Saturn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega_Saturn "Sega Saturn"). The game takes place within the context of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). The game is played as either [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"), [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany"), or [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan"). The outcomes of certain battles result in a change in the path of the war.

_[Electronic Gaming Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic\_Gaming\_Monthly "Electronic Gaming Monthly")_ gave the game an 8 out of 10, praising the ability to view the outcome of each battle in cinematic view and the use of experience points.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisenryaku#cite_note-14)

_**Daisenryaku Portable**_ is a military [turn-based strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn-based_strategy "Turn-based strategy") game for the [PlayStation Portable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Portable "PlayStation Portable") published by [Genki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genki_(company) "Genki (company)").

The game takes place on a battlefield of hexagons, upon which the player assembles an army. The aim is to capture cities and factories, in order to increase available resources. The hexagons are rendered in an isometric view with no rotation supported, but the individual attacks are rendered in 3D. The game supports use of the ad hoc wireless mode of the PlayStation Portable for two player multiplayer.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisenryaku#cite_note-16) The main campaign takes place in the [Far East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_East "Far East") in the twenty-first century. The factions in the game are fictional versions of Japan, USA, Russia, China, South Korea, and North Korea.

_**Daisenryaku Perfect: Senjō no Hasha**_ is a turn-based military simulation set in the modern era. The player can selects from 22 countries and then becomes either an ally or attacking country.

While the vast majority of titles in the _Daisenryaku_ series were never released outside of Japan, a few titles were officially localized and others were partially translated by fans.

The series saw its first release in English when [Working Designs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_Designs "Working Designs") partnered with SystemSoft Alpha to translate and release _[Iron Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron\_Storm\_(1995\_video\_game) "Iron Storm (1995 video game)")_ for the Sega Saturn in North America in 1996.

A second entry in English was realized when [Kemco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemco "Kemco") released _[Dai Senryaku VII: Modern Military Tactics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dai\_Senryaku\_VII:\_Modern\_Military\_Tactics "Dai Senryaku VII: Modern Military Tactics")_ in North America for the Microsoft Xbox in 2005. The PlayStation 2 port also saw a North American release in 2007.

The [mecha musume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecha_musume "Mecha musume") spin-off of Daisenryaku, _Moe moe 2-ji Daisen (ryaku)_, was translated into Chinese and released by Taiwanese publisher TWTTIME Technology Co., Ltd for the PC in 2008.

_Daisenryaku Perfect 4.0_ saw a worldwide release on Steam in 2018 with a partial English translation done by SystemSoft themselves. Users have criticized the translation calling it "[machine translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_translation "Machine translation")" and many units' names remain in Japanese.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisenryaku#cite_note-23)

Fans have partially translated various entries in the series. _Advanced Daisenryaku_ for the Sega Mega Drive,[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisenryaku#cite_note-24)_Advanced Daisenryaku 2001_ for the Sega Dreamcast,[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisenryaku#cite_note-25) and _Daisenryaku VII_ for the PC [[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisenryaku#cite_note-26) all saw translations of various levels released unofficially.
